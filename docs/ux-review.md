# UX review: Customer Review Analyzer (live app, `main`)

Reviewer: Wren (`ux-designer-apple-hig`) · Date: 2026-11-21 · Status: recommendations only (no live files edited)

## Summary

The app explains itself honestly. Every result carries an NLTK or spaCy badge, the charts have text alternatives, and the live analyzer starts in about 4 s with staged progress. **On a phone, though, people struggle to find and reach the main tasks.** Two of the four tabs are off-screen and the navigation scrolls away. The "Which library…?" legend repeats above every tab and pushes the real content below the first screen. Several things also work only with a mouse or at default text size.

**Fix the top 3 first.** Together they cover first visit, navigation and the live analyzer for every phone user:

1. **Phone navigation hides half the app.** "Analyze your own review" is cut off and "About & method" is fully off-screen in a non-sticky strip with no overflow cue (#1).
2. **The library legend repeats on every tab and fills 47% of the phone's first screen.** The dashboard's first number starts at 785 px and the live textarea at 970 px on an 844 px screen (#2).
3. **Chart drill-down needs a mouse.** "Select a bar to read those reviews" can't be reached by keyboard or screen reader (#3).

**Scope:** first visit to the dashboard; Reviews & examples drill-down (leaderboard → filtered list → review detail, curated examples, empty filter); Analyze your own review (cold start, result, empty submit, forced load error); About & method. Phone 390×844 and desktop 1280×800, light and dark, all with `reducedMotion: 'reduce'`; `contrast: 'more'` checked in both schemes; 200% text at 320 px.

**Method:** Nielsen's 10 heuristics (skill `usability-heuristic-review`), HIG principles (hierarchy, consistency, clarity, feedback; skill `hig-principles-review`) and the `accessibility-audit` checklist. All numbers were measured with Playwright (Chromium) against `python3 -m http.server 8811 -d site`. Contrast was computed from computed colours, with translucent cells composited over their panel. Target sizes come from `getBoundingClientRect()`. The text-zoom test sets `document.documentElement.style.fontSize = '200%'` at 320 px width. Screenshots are in [`ux-review-assets/`](ux-review-assets/) at deviceScaleFactor 1.

Knowledge used: `knowledge/ux-designer-apple-hig/01-accessibility.md` (HIG Accessibility, <https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility>), `02-layout.md` (<https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout>), `03-tab-bars.md` (<https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars>), `04-sidebars.md`, `05-materials-for-ux.md`, `06-web-preference-media.md` (MDN `prefers-*`), and examples 01 and 02.

## What works (keep it)

- **Honest NLTK/spaCy labelling.** Every panel header carries a source badge (NLTK, spaCy, or NLTK + spaCy) with a text label and ≥ 7.5:1 contrast. The live tab says plainly that spaCy isn't available for typed text, and the spaCy placeholder panel links to where it is. That meets the `netlify-guardrail` and `ux-writing` rules.
- **Fast cold start with real feedback.** Status moves from "Loading the Python runtime (Pyodide, about 13 MB on first visit)…" to "Warming up the analyzer…" to "NLTK 3.10.4 is ready (loaded in 3.7–4.3 s)". Pyodide warms up as soon as the tab opens, a warm analysis takes 70 ms, and the textarea stays editable throughout.
- **Accessible charts.** Each chart has a data-specific `aria-label` and a "Show data table" disclosure, uses a CVD-safe blue/grey/orange palette, and switches off animation under reduce motion.
- **A correct ARIA tabs pattern** with roving `tabindex`, arrow, Home and End keys, and linkable `#hash` tabs.
- **Leaderboards drill into reviews** through real buttons with descriptive `aria-label`s and a removable filter chip. This is the model to copy for #3.
- **A rich review detail.** Entity highlights with label superscripts (≥ 11.9:1), entity-masked sentiment, sentence-by-sentence scores and an aspect–opinion table: a clear story about combining the two libraries.
- **Solid dark mode tokens** and a reflow that works at 320 px at default text size.
- **A good empty submit.** "Type or paste a review first." is announced, styled as an error (5.43 / 7.55:1), and focus returns to the textarea. Add `aria-invalid="true"` too.

### Option A: "Liquid Glass"
- **Glass only on the navigation layer** (bottom tab bar, header or sidebar). Charts, cards, review text, the live result and the badges sit on **solid** surfaces (note 05: glass in the content layer "creates a confusing visual hierarchy").
- **Regular variant** for the tab bar: label text ≥ 4.5:1 **over the worst-case content scrolling beneath it**. Test it over the blue and orange chart bars and the purple spaCy badges, in both schemes.
- An opaque fallback that triggers under `prefers-reduced-transparency: reduce` **and** `prefers-contrast: more` **and** `@supports not (backdrop-filter: blur(1px))`. Chromium can't emulate reduced transparency, so add a test class.
- The selected tab can't be signalled by tint alone: use fill + weight + `aria-selected`. Use no blur or scale animation when switching tabs (HIG: avoid "animating into and out of blurs"), and fades of 150 ms or less.
- `main` needs bottom padding equal to the bar height + `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)` so the last card and the Analyze button never sit under the floating bar.
- Use one or two glass surfaces at most, never stacked (header glass + tab-bar glass + sticky filter glass is too much).

### Option B: "Clean Apple Light"
- The risk is **faint greys.** Apple-style `label-3` (60% alpha) and hairline separators fail here. Keep secondary text ≥ 4.5:1 (`label-2` at .78 alpha is about the floor) and **control borders ≥ 3:1** (fixes #6).
- Use inset grouped cards (16 px padding, 12–16 px radius, 24–32 px between groups), one large title per screen, and 16 px phone gutters.
- Pastel badges on white must still reach 4.5:1. The current tokens (7.5–8.9:1) are a good baseline; don't lighten them.
- **Still ship a full dark mode** and increase contrast. "Light" is the look, not the only scheme.
- Don't use a segmented control for top-level navigation. Segmented controls switch views within a screen, so use a tab bar or sidebar for the 4 sections.

### Option C: "Dark Pro"
- Respect the system setting. Dark can be the showcase, but light-preference users must get a working light theme (or the option must state the trade-off in the comparison table).
- **Fix the heat map first** (#9: current dark cells reach 3.15:1). Check the sentiment colours as graphics ≥ 3:1 and as text ≥ 4.5:1 on every surface level (base, elevated and grouped).
- Show elevation with lighter surfaces, not shadows. Avoid pure-black backgrounds behind saturated bars (halation), and keep the focus ring ≥ 3:1 against all surface levels.
- Dense "pro" layouts tempt small controls. Keep 44 px targets and 17 px body, and in a sidebar layout keep at most two levels with nothing critical at the bottom.
- Badges: light text on a dark tinted pill, ≥ 4.5:1. The current dark tokens (9.4–10.2:1) work. Make sure NLTK green and spaCy purple stay distinguishable to colour-blind users through their **labels**, not their hue.

**Comparison criteria for the `/options/` index:** navigation pattern (phone and desktop) · tab labels (word count) · badge contrast (light / dark / more) · what sits above the fold at 390×844 on Dashboard and Analyze · 200% text result · reduced-transparency and increase-contrast screenshots · effort to adopt.

## Open risks / not verified

- Tested in Chromium only. iOS Safari (real safe areas, `backdrop-filter`, text-size settings) and VoiceOver/TalkBack were not tested by hand.
- Browser page zoom at 200% was approximated by the 320 px reflow test plus root font-size 200%. Real "text size only" settings in Safari and Firefox may differ slightly.
- The cold start was measured locally (3.7–4.3 s). On a slow 4G connection the 13 MB download will take far longer, and the 120 s timeout path was not exercised.
- `prefers-reduced-transparency` can't be emulated in Playwright. That doesn't matter today (the app has no translucency) but it will for Option A.
- `site/options/` was still being built in parallel and was not reviewed, so the option guidance is written ahead of the designs.
